# 2026-10-29 — The oracle's voice: what the reading borrows, and from where

His ask (2026-10-29): an "epic/elder tone… like an omenic sage on the TV"; not Walter Mercado;
the Brujo Mayor only as the opposite of that pomp; "an occidental sorcerer magic hat", not a
Mexican shaman (a limpia with herbs and the mal de ojo are fine). A web search, no checker pass:
every row says what the cited page itself shows. No writing guide gives Spanish formulas; the
traits come from the texts.

| Source | What the page shows | Used as (ADR 0027) |
|---|---|---|
| Delphi: Croesus and the "great empire" (Herodotus I 53, 90); the "wooden walls" before Salamis ([El Español](https://elespanol.com/historia/20240515/ombligo-mundo-antigua-grecia-hoy-todavia-puede-visitar/855164842_0.html), [UCM](https://revistas.ucm.es/index.php/CFCG/article/download/41551/39628/57350)) | The answer named no empire; Croesus assumed it was Persia's; ambiguity kept the shrine right | At most one line with two readings |
| Macbeth's witches, Act IV.1 ([SparkNotes ES](https://www.sparknotes.com/shakespeare/es/la-tragedia-de-macbeth/section7/), [LitCharts](https://www.litcharts.com/lit/macbeth/questions/what-are-the-witches-three-prophecies-in-macbeth)) | "Ninguno nacido de mujer", Birnam wood: true, sideways | The equivocation, a condition that comes true |
| Geoffrey of Monmouth's *Prophetiae Merlini*, c. 1135 ([World History](https://www.worldhistory.org/trans/es/1-18148/merlin/)) | Animal emblems for houses and kings (the boar of Cornwall, the dragons) | The sign read as its emblem |
