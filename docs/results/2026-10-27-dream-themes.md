# 2026-10-27: the fortune act's dream themes, their prevalence and their sources

Desktop, branch `sorting-hat`, this change (`fish_brain/fortune.py`, `fish_brain/fortune_bank.py`).
The 21 themes of `fortune.DREAM_THEMES`, with the figures the design pass of 2026-10-27 gathered
(three web sweeps merged into `scratchpad/dreams.json`; no repository file quoted a number from
them before this one). The hat's omen lines (`fortune_bank.DREAM_LINES`) are drafts in the adult
register, his to edit; the figures below say only how often a theme comes up, never what it means.

No Spanish-language or Latin American replication of the Typical Dreams Questionnaire was found:
every Mexican number is search volume or a reader survey, and says so.

## The themes, in the bank's order

Prevalence spine: Nielsen et al. 2003 (1,181 Canadian students, 55-item Typical Dreams
Questionnaire, lifetime), then Germany (Schredl et al. 2004) and Hong Kong (Yu 2008) via the same
table, the all-ages study (Maggiolini et al. 2020, ages 8-70) and the most-recent-dream study
(Mathes et al. 2014). Mexico: Morning Life 2022 search volumes (147 countries), Google México
weekly searches (El Financiero, May 2016) and the iWay Magazine reader survey (2,500 readers,
March 2026, self-described as not representative).

## The keyword policy (`fortune.dream_theme`)

- Keywords are folded (no accents, lower case, ñ as n, the transcript's own form) and matched
  as whole words or phrases; a keyword ending in `*` is a stem (`persegu*` hears "perseguían",
  "persiguiendo"). Mexican forms first: víbora, alacrán, camión, carro, temblor, alberca,
  "me correteaban", chimuelo, michi, lomito, abuelita, finado, "se me apareció".
- Every keyword belongs to one theme and, heard alone, lands on it (`test_fortune` checks all
  of them); the collisions the research listed are resolved by the tie rule (first mentioned,
  then the longer phrase): "corría lento" is `tarde`, "perdí el camión" is `tarde`, "era bruja"
  is `volar`, "ratón de los dientes" is `dientes`. "La casa de mi abuela" and "no encontraba a
  mi mamá" went to the family: with adults, `seres_queridos` is the better line for both.
- `dientes` and `seres_queridos` weigh 2 (teeth and the departed sit far above their Canadian
  rank for a Mexican audience): "se me caían los dientes" beats `caer`, "mi abuelita que ya
  murió me perseguía" beats `perseguido`.
- Chase words beat the violence words of `otro` (a killer behind you is still a chase);
  any content beats `nada`; `nada` is said only when no dream was told.
- No theme's words heard: None, and the act asks once more with the themes listed; a second
  miss stores `otro`, whose line never engages with the content.
